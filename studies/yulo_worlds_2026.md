## 1. The question this note closes

Can a chart say anything about a competition result?

Not "did the markers fit." They did, once. The question is whether the method can
reach this class of question at all.

## 3. Why one hit out of two is not a result

The same markers were present on the gold and on the fourth place. By the Guide's own
rule, a marker found on both the winning day and the losing day explains neither.
That is not a weak finding. It is no finding.

## 4. The structural problem, which is the actual conclusion

A competition result is not a property of one person's day. It is relative. Whoever
wins beats everyone else who competed that day. So a chart read in isolation cannot
speak to placement, however well its markers seem to fit, because placement depends
on people the chart does not contain.

The method would need the field: every competitor's chart, read the same way, compared
on the same day.

**The field cannot be built.** Those competitors have not asked. Private people's charts
run only when they ask (Guide, No harm). So the comparison the method requires is one
that cannot be made without breaking the rule the practice rests on.

This is not a gap waiting on better data or a cleverer rule. The data is closed by
design, and the design is correct.

## 5. What this does not say

- It does not say the markers meant nothing on those days.
- It does not say the daily layer is useless. It says the daily layer cannot be
  tested against a competitive placement.
- It does not rule on whether a chart speaks to a person's own experience of a day,
  which is a different question and still open.

## 6. Ruling

**The competition-result question is closed.** Not unresolved, closed: the test that
would settle it cannot be run within the practice's own limits.

No further Yulo runs are planned. L-004 stays blocked. L-005 is retired unrun.

If the question ever reopens, it reopens on a field whose subjects have asked, or
not at all.
