### 0.1c delta — what changed and why

Four adjudication rounds produced zero located classical citations. Every genuinely classical question ended at *the doctrine exists and I cannot point to it*. That is an access constraint, not a reasoning failure, and no further adjudication will change it.

0.1c therefore does two things. It **closes what convergence closed** — three register items resolved by blind agreement across instruments. And it **makes the remainder a reported output rather than a hidden deferral**: every run now ends with an Escalation Report naming what the engine could not settle on that specific chart, what each possible resolution would mean, and what would resolve it.

The governing principle: **an unresolved condition, correctly named, is a finding.** It is not a failure of the reading and it is not a caveat appended to one. A specification that cannot say *I do not know* will say something else instead, and that something else will be wrong at a rate nobody can measure.

## 7A. THE ESCALATION REPORT [0.1c]

**Mandatory. Every run. No exceptions.**

The Ledger asks what time will tell. The Escalation Report states what the specification could not settle — on *this* chart, in *this* field, with the consequences that follow.

### 7A.1 What goes in it

Only conditions **actually encountered in this run.** The engine does not recite the full register. A condition that had no instance in this field is not reported.

Classify every encountered condition:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FIRED** | The condition arose and its resolution affects the reading |
| **BOUNDED** | The condition arose, but every candidate resolution produces the same finding here. Checked and closed for this chart |
| **INERT** | The condition was checked and had no instance in this field. Report only if the check is non-obvious |

### 7A.2 Consequence tier

Every FIRED condition carries a tier. This is what lets a reader triage the report honestly.

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **GRADE** | The candidate resolutions produce different Triage grades. Highest priority |
| **PRESCRIPTION** | Same grade, different Shield prescription or window |
| **RECORD** | Neither grade nor prescription changes. Logged for completeness and for cross-chart accumulation |

### 7A.3 Required format per FIRED condition

1. **Plain name** of the condition — what structure in this chart is unsettled
2. **Register ID** and the rule that flagged it
3. **Candidate resolutions**, each with what it produces for this chart
4. **What both candidates agree on** — the common ground that stands regardless
5. **Consequence tier**
6. **What would resolve it** — a page-verified citation, an operator ruling, a record observation, or a fixture

Item 4 is not optional. A reader given only the disagreement will conclude that nothing is known. Stating the common ground is what makes an escalation informative rather than merely uncertain.

### 7A.4 Clean runs

Where nothing fired, the section reads in full:

> *"No unresolved conditions arose in this field. Every interaction resolved under a settled rule."*

It is never omitted. Its absence is indistinguishable from an oversight.

### 7A.5 Client-facing register

For readings delivered to a person rather than to the bench, the same content is rendered in plain language. **Register IDs, rule numbers, and tier labels do not appear.** Template:

> **What this reading could not settle**
>
> Your chart contains [N] structure(s) where the classical sources give more than one defensible answer, and where the difference would change what this reading says. They are listed below, with what each possibility would mean.
>
> This is not a weakness in your chart. It is the limit of what can be established with certainty from the classical record — and naming it is part of reading honestly rather than reading confidently.
>
> ---
>
> **[Plain name of the structure]**
>
> Two readings are possible: [A] or [B].
>
> If [A]: [what it would mean, in plain terms].
> If [B]: [what it would mean, in plain terms].
>
> **What both readings agree on:** [the common ground].
>
> **How this may become clearer:** [record observation with its window, or classical verification].

Only GRADE and PRESCRIPTION tier items appear in the client register. RECORD-tier items stay on the bench — they are specification maintenance, not information a person needs about their own life.

### 7A.6 Accumulation across charts [0.1c]

Every run's Escalation Report is logged. Per register item, the log carries: **times encountered · times FIRED · times GRADE-tier · times PRESCRIPTION-tier.**

This is what sets verification priority. A condition that fires rarely and never changes a grade may stay flagged indefinitely at no cost. A condition that fires on most charts and moves the verdict is where classical verification is owed, and the log — not the accident of which chart surfaced it first — decides which text to open.

**Standing note:** the register's current contents were derived from a single chart across four adjudication rounds. That chart is an acknowledged exception rather than a representative case. Until the log carries a meaningful population, the register reflects one chart's structure and should not be read as a map of where the specification is generally weak.
